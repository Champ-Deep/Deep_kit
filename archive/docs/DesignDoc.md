02 // MEET THE MASCOT: THE CHILL LEGEND ("THE DEEP")

2.1 Just Chillin’

The mascot is a tiny, pixel-perfect version of Sreedeep. But here’s the kicker: he’s the anti-Clippy. Unlike those annoying AI assistants that won't stop talking, interrupting your flow, or popping up with "helpful" tips you never asked for, The Chill Legend is just a silent legend. He’s like the "Verified" badge for your local tech stack. He represents the human curator who stayed up all night, fought with the Docker configs, and optimized the memory leaks so you didn't have to. He’s there to let you know everything is under control and the "high score" is within reach.

2.2 What He Actually Does (The Chill Protocol)

The Deep follows a strict "Non-Interference Directive." He exists to provide comfort through presence, not noise.

The Legit Check: If you see him chilling in the header, it's a signal. It means that specific tool is 100% optimized, containerized, and verified safe to run on your machine. He’s the seal of local quality.

The High-Five: He pops up in those "Success" popups (the Toasts) to give you a digital nod. Whether an automation just finished its 1,000th run or a massive Docker pull finally hit 100%, he's there to celebrate the win with you in a localized, non-obstructive way.

The Silent Guide: He never talks. No chat bubbles, no notifications. He’s a cool visual anchor. His presence tells the user: "Relax, the tech is handled. The server is humming. You just focus on being creative and playing the game."

2.3 The Visual Specs (Arcade Edition)

The Style: 8-bit or 16-bit pixel art. We’re talking blocky, cutesy, and totally approachable. No complex tech circuits or scary cybernetic implants. Just a chill guy in a blazer.

The Lines: We use thick, high-contrast outlines (3px minimum). Why? Because we’re slapping heavy CRT scanline filters over the UI, and thin lines just get eaten alive by the flicker. He needs to stay sharp even when the screen is mimicking a 1985 arcade cabinet.

Dynamic Colors: He’s a chameleon. He isn't stuck in one color; he inherits the active theme color. If you're in the Cyber Teal hub, he's Teal. If you're running LLMs in Phosphor Green, he glows Green. He is the ghost in the machine, but a friendly one.

Home Base: You’ll usually find him parked in the top-left of the main Hubs or chilling as the lead icon in the bottom-right Success Toasts.

04 // THE PAINT JOB: ARCADE CABINET OVERLAYS

Every app in the arsenal (Strapi, n8n, Neo4j) is "reskinned" to look like a dedicated arcade cabinet. We're skinning the engine, not rebuilding it. We use CSS injection to force our aesthetic onto third-party tools.

4.1 The CSS Injection Rules

Every app gets a deepkit-overlay.css injected into its container. This file forces the UI to respect the brand:

The Font: JetBrains Mono everywhere. It’s the king of technical legibility. If you can't get that, Courier New is the only fallback. Absolutely no sans-serif fonts allowed—ever. If it doesn't look like code, it's not DeepKit.

Hard Edges: Keep it mechanical. border-radius: 0 !important. No "bubbly" or "soft" here. We want sharp, industrial lines that feel like they were milled out of steel or molded from 80s plastic.

The Scanlines: A global 5% opacity "CRT texture" over the whole screen. It’s a repeating linear-gradient that makes the digital screen feel like physical hardware. This adds a layer of "grit" that makes the clean neon pop.

4.2 The User Experience (UX) Feel

The Snappy Ingest: Text shouldn't just "load" like a boring webpage. It should stream in at a blistering 5ms per character. It makes the system feel "alive" and reinforces the fact that your local CPU is a beast that's processing data faster than you can think.

Stay Out the Way: Success popups (Toasts) always go in the bottom-right corner. We never show full-screen success modals or "loading" spinners that hijack the mouse. Let the user keep their flow; just give 'em a quick nod in the periphery that the level is complete.

05 // SOUNDS: THE 8-BIT ORCHESTRA 🔊

DeepKit needs to feel tactile. We use "Haptic Sound" to confirm your reality. It's the difference between a cheap touchscreen and a high-end mechanical keyboard.

THE CLICK: A snappy, 8-bit "blip" whenever you hit a button. It's the sound of picking an item in a retro RPG menu. It provides immediate sensory feedback that your intent was registered by the machine.

THE CHIME: A happy little 3-note ascending "Level Up" jingle when a task finishes. It's the "Dopamine Hit" for finishing a workflow.

THE SWEEP: A tiny, low-pass "whoosh" when you switch themes. It’s subtle, but it makes the transition feel like a physical sliding mechanism within the arcade cabinet.

THE BUZZ: A flat, short "bzzzt" (100Hz) if something breaks or a port is blocked. It’s a warning, not a panic button. It tells you "Hey, check the config, something's not plugged in right."

06 // PORT RANGES: THE ADDRESS BOOK

We’ve grouped the ports so you can navigate the Arsenal like different "Bays" in a giant tech hangar. If you know the number range, you know the function. This keeps the no-code users from getting overwhelmed by the 25+ services.

3000s [MAIN_HUB]: The "Face" of the system. These are your dashboards, CRMs, and CMS interfaces. If you're managing things, you're in the 3000s. Color: Cyber Teal.

5000s [WORKSHOP]: The "Engine" room. This is where the actual work happens. n8n and all your workflow logic live here. Color: Cyber Teal.

6000s-7000s [THE VAULT]: The "Storage" layer. This is where the knowledge lives. Postgres, Redis, Qdrant, and Graph databases. Color: Ghost Mono or Bright Amber.

11000+ [THE LAB]: The "Brain." This is the AI core. Ollama, runtimes, and local inference models. This is where the heavy compute happens. Color: Phosphor Green.

03 // ARCHITECTURE: PORT CONNECTIVITY

We have moved away from the "Stage" model to a Port-Based Architecture, reflecting DeepKit’s modular, interconnected nature.

3.1 Port Logic

In-Ports (Left/Top): Handles data ingestion, user input, and triggers.

Process Hub (Center): The central logic area where the Nexus resides during operations.

Out-Ports (Right/Bottom): Handles exports, visualizations, and API handoffs.

Magnetic Snapping: Components "snap" into Ports with a magnetic animation effect using a cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1) easing profile.
